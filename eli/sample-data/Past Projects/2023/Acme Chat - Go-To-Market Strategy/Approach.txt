ACME CHAT GO-TO-MARKET - APPROACH AND METHODOLOGY

Workplan (12 weeks)
Weeks 1-2   Kickoff, data requests, interview guide, hypotheses
Weeks 3-6   Customer and prospect interviews, win/loss analysis, competitive teardown
Weeks 5-8   Market sizing and channel economics model
Weeks 9-10  Synthesis, recommendation workshop with the VP of Growth
Weeks 11-12 Final presentation, 90-day launch plan, handover

Interview sampling
We stratified interviews by company size (200-500 and 500-1,000 employees), by status (active paid,
free workspace, churned, lost deal) and by role (IT admin, team lead, executive sponsor). Acme Chat's
customer success team made introductions to active accounts. For churned and lost deals we used
the alumni network and cold outreach, which yielded a 14% response rate.

Hypotheses we tested
H1  Mid-market buyers decide per team, then standardise company-wide.
H2  Security and compliance, not price, is the main reason deals stall.
H3  A sales-assist motion triggered by product usage beats cold outbound for this segment.
H4  Integrations are the strongest predictor of retention.

H1, H3 and H4 held up. H2 held up for companies above 500 employees.

Market sizing
Bottom-up: number of companies in the size band, multiplied by the share with 3 or more active
chat workspaces, multiplied by average seats per company, multiplied by blended price per seat.
We cross-checked against analyst estimates and landed within 12%.

What we would do differently
- Start the security-review interviews earlier. They changed the recommendation more than anything else.
- Ask for product analytics access in week one.
