# Acme Chat: Enterprise Pricing and Packaging

**Term:** Fall 2025 | **Team:** 5 consultants | **Industry:** B2B SaaS, team messaging
**Follow-on to:** Acme Chat - Go-To-Market Strategy (2023)

> Fictional case written for the sample data set. Acme Chat is not a real company.

## Situation

After the 2023 mid-market launch, Acme Chat's enterprise pipeline grew, but discounting did too. The
average enterprise deal closed at a 34% discount to list price, and no two large customers paid the same
rate. The CRO asked for a pricing and packaging structure that supports enterprise deals without giving
away the self-serve base.

## Approach

- Analysed 640 closed deals for discount depth, seat count, term length and add-ons.
- Willingness-to-pay survey of 210 IT and finance buyers using a conjoint exercise.
- Teardown of how five collaboration vendors package security, compliance and AI features.

## Findings

- **Per-seat pricing alone leaks value.** Accounts with heavy bot and integration usage generated 3x the
  message volume per seat but paid the same price.
- **Buyers will pay for control.** Audit logs, retention policies and data residency were the three most
  valued enterprise features, ahead of AI summaries.
- **Discounts track negotiation, not value.** Seat count explained only 18% of the variance in discount depth.
- **Annual commitments are cheap to win.** Moving from monthly to annual billing cost an average of 8 points
  of discount, far lower than the 17 points given for multi-year terms.

## Recommendations

1. Move to three packages (Team, Business, Enterprise) with fences built on controls and scale, not seat
   count alone.
2. Add a usage-based component for automation and API traffic above a generous included allowance.
3. Publish a discount matrix with approval thresholds so deal desk can say yes quickly.
4. Offer a 12-month commitment discount by default and cap multi-year concessions at 10 points.

## Expected impact

Model projects 6 to 9% net revenue uplift on new enterprise deals and a 20-point reduction in discount
variance across comparable customers. Pricing changes should roll out first to new customers, with an
opt-in migration for existing accounts at renewal.
