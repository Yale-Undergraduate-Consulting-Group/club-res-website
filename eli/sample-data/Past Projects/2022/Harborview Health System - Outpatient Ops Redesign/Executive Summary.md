# Harborview Health System: Outpatient Operations Redesign

**Term:** Spring 2022 | **Team:** 4 consultants | **Industry:** Healthcare operations

> Fictional case written for the sample data set. Harborview Health System is not a real organization.

## Situation

Harborview runs eleven outpatient clinics. Patients waited an average of 31 days for a new-patient
appointment, 17% of booked visits ended as no-shows, and physicians reported finishing charts after
hours. Leadership wanted to add capacity without hiring.

## Approach

- Shadowed front-desk staff and medical assistants at three clinics for two weeks each.
- Mapped patient flow from scheduling call to checkout and timed every handoff.
- Analysed 14 months of scheduling data to separate true capacity limits from template rules.

## Findings

- **Scheduling templates were the bottleneck, not physician time.** Slots were blocked for visit types
  that no longer existed, hiding about 9% of weekly capacity.
- **No-shows were predictable.** Patients who booked more than 21 days out were three times as likely
  to miss the visit. A reminder text 48 hours ahead recovered few of them, but a short waitlist did.
- **Rooming took twice as long as the clinical visit** at two of the three observed sites, driven by
  duplicated intake questions.
- **Discharge paperwork** was completed after the patient left in 40% of visits.

## Recommendations

1. Rebuild visit-type templates with clinic leads and release blocked capacity.
2. Introduce an overbook rule for long-lead appointments and a same-day waitlist.
3. Standardise intake in the patient portal so medical assistants only verify answers.
4. Pilot a pre-visit chart-prep checklist to cut after-hours charting.

## Outcome

The pilot clinic cut new-patient wait times from 31 to 19 days within one quarter. Harborview adopted
the template rebuild across all eleven clinics the following year.
