# Outreach pipeline

This is the operational contract for finding contacts, preparing personalized mail, sending from member accounts, and reporting outcomes.

## Member path

1. **Target lists** select companies from the club prospect dataset.
2. **People** records proposed contacts, evidence, and keep/drop decisions. Kept contacts link directly into Drafts with the target-list filter preserved.
3. **Find contacts** creates a durable, member-owned company search. Website and permitted provider evidence are merged, addresses are normalized, and mail routing is checked. Import moves accepted results into the club contact catalog.
4. **Drafts** stores drafts under the current member. A bulk template must use explicit fields such as `{{first_name}}` or `{{company}}`; otherwise the open editor is applied only to its current recipient and other recipients keep their own saved drafts.
5. **Campaigns** copies one subject and body per recipient. The owner previews, repairs, or removes each recipient before release.
6. **Release** freezes the sender, recipient, content, signature, and follow-up steps. Subsequent contact edits cannot retarget a released message. After that transaction commits, the API starts one background drain immediately; the five-minute scheduler remains the fallback. Both use the same conditional dispatch claim, so a race can claim each immutable intent only once.
7. **Dispatch** pages through the pending queue until it fills its small batch or exhausts the campaign; held rows at the front cannot hide sendable recipients behind them. A row is claimed transactionally only inside the recipient's local weekday send window (default 08:00–17:00, `SEND_WINDOW_HOURS`), derived from the company register's US state or UK country and otherwise defaulting to US Eastern. Outside-window rows stay pending. Gmail provider timeouts remain quarantined for reconciliation; a rejection before submission is safe to retry after the member fixes Gmail access.
8. **Results** attaches opens, replies, delivery delays, and bounces to the exact outbound message. Open pixels are approximate; replies and Gmail delivery notifications are stronger evidence.

## Address confidence

The application can prove address syntax and whether a domain has a mail route without sending mail. Optional SMTP `RCPT TO` probing never issues `DATA`, but many corporate servers block probes or accept every recipient. Those outcomes remain **mailbox unconfirmed**. Only a later delivery, bounce, or reply supplies operational mailbox evidence.

Before the first send to an unproven mail host, a guessed or web-only address uses the configured Verifalia check when external verification is enabled and has free-plan capacity. A provider-confirmed nonexistent mailbox is not sent: its campaign row fails with `Mailbox does not exist (Verifalia)`, the contact is marked dead, and the address is suppressed. Deliverable and catch-all results continue through the existing one-host-probe gate. Disabled, exhausted, unavailable, or inconclusive external verification does not add a new hold.

Provider answers are **club-wide**. Every Verifalia result, whoever asked (a pre-send check, the roster pass, or the background job), is stored once per address in `mailbox_verdicts` (outreach migration 4) and reused by every member for `VERIFALIA_RESPONSE_CACHE_DAYS` (default and maximum 90; an inconclusive answer is kept 7 days). `assess_address` reads that row before it reserves a credit, so one address costs the club one credit per window. A verdict also updates `email_verification_status` on every contact and prospect with that address: confirmed → `valid`, catch-all → `likely_valid`, rejected → `dead` (contacts) or `invalid` (prospects); rows already `dead` or `invalid` are not upgraded. `proof_for` in `services/mailbox_verdicts.py` folds verdicts with real mail evidence into one level per address: `replied` > `bounced` > `mailbox_rejected` > `delivered` > `mailbox_confirmed` > `catch_all` > `unverified` (an expired verdict counts as `unverified`; a suppression with no bounce behind it counts as `mailbox_rejected`).

The `mailbox_backlog` job (leader only, every 15 minutes, at most 25 credits per tick) runs only when Verifalia is switched on. It spends the day's remaining credits less `VERIFALIA_PRESEND_RESERVE` (default 5), so pre-send checks always have credits left. The daily budget is `VERIFALIA_DAILY_CREDIT_CAP` (default 25, the free plan; hard ceiling 250, the $9/month plan). The queue is deduplicated by lowercase address and ordered: pending recipients of draft, paused and releasing campaigns (releasing first); then shared-catalog contacts (no owner, higher confidence first); then discovery prospects (newest run, then score). It skips addresses whose proof is anything but `unverified`, addresses with an unexpired verdict, suppressed or already dead/invalid ones, junk-reviewed rows, and role, consumer and disposable addresses. A provider error or exhausted day ends the tick. Checks run as actor 0, a reservation owner with no user row, so nobody can sign in as it. Outreach migration 4 also rebuilds the one-row-a-day credit table with the wider 0–250 allowance check; the old per-actor `verification_cache` table is no longer written or read.

A scraped or web-found address is an observed sample of a company's format, not a verified sample: an MX record proves only that the domain accepts mail. A format becomes proven only after one address survives the configured mailbox-proof grace period without a bounce, a person replies, or a member explicitly states the format. Gmail sync records that no-bounce delivery evidence once per outbound message. A later permanent bounce removes that address's proof, lowers the format confidence, suppresses the address, and keeps the company's remaining unproven recipients held. Outreach migration 3 recalculates the old scrape-inflated verified counts from retained delivery, reply, and bounce evidence.

Member labels state the evidence rather than a blanket validity judgment: **Seen on the web**, **Format guessed**, **Catch-all domain**, **Mailbox confirmed** (provider assessment), **Delivered before**, **Replied**, and **Bounced**. **Domain accepts mail** remains explicitly domain-only.

The UI and API must not label MX, inferred patterns, AI output, or an accepted SMTP probe as inbox verified.

This matches the boundary in the SMTP standard: receiving systems may disable `VRFY` for privacy and return `252` when they cannot verify a user. YAMM likewise learns its useful states after a campaign is sent: its official tracking report covers opens, clicks, replies, bounces, and unsubscribes. The club implementation therefore uses local syntax, DNS/MX, source evidence, and optional no-`DATA` probing before send, then uses the tracking pixel and the member's Gmail metadata after send. See [RFC 5321](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5321/), [YAMM tracking documentation](https://yamm.com/help/), and [Google's Gmail sending guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126).

## Scale and ownership

- Contacts without an owner are shared club catalog records. An assigned contact is visible to its member and administrators.
- Drafts, discovery runs, campaigns, Gmail credentials, immutable dispatches, and reconciliation are member-bound.
- Duplicate additions resolve to one campaign recipient. A recipient can produce only one initial dispatch key.
- One failed or inactive sender cannot stop other members' scheduled campaign batches.
- Initial sends and follow-ups reserve against the same member-wide daily ceiling: the member's own limit in Settings, never above `CAMPAIGN_DAILY_SEND_LIMIT` (default 100), counting uncertain sends. `dispatch_service.claim` enforces it, so parallel campaigns cannot multiply the allowance and a held follow-up simply waits for the next run.
- The initial-send drain and follow-up job use the same recipient-local Monday–Friday window. `SEND_WINDOW_HOURS` defaults to `8-17`; `off` disables the window. US register states map to the predominant state zone (Arizona has its no-DST zone), GB maps to `Europe/London`, and unknown companies use `America/New_York`.
- Follow-ups (`follow_up_job`, hourly at :10) go only to contacts already mailed, so a campaign in `needs_attention` (the mailbox-proof hold concerns unsent rows) or still `releasing` keeps following up; a `paused` campaign stops them. A sequence also stops for a contact who replied (an opt-out reply is a reply) or whose address bounced. When the latest automatic reply (out of office) is newer than two business days, the next step waits until two business days after it; no return date is parsed because only the reply's headers are stored. A due step is sent only while the recipient's local time is inside the send window (`send_window.open_now`), otherwise it stays queued for a later hourly run.
- Every email (campaign, follow-up, Drafts test send, invitation) goes out from the sending member's own connected Gmail under the member's own name: the From display name is the Profile sign-off name, else their Google name, the same name the sign-off prints. It is never "YUCG Outreach"; with no name on file the From is the bare address. `gmail_api._sender_name` decides it for all three Gmail helpers.
- The club-wide Bedrock reservation ceiling also covers discovery ranking, so contact research cannot bypass the inference budget used by Studio.
- Discovery runs use the SQLite-backed queue. One active run per member and a bounded club queue protect the single EC2 host. Expiring fenced leases prevent overlapping workers from writing the same run; inactive members are rejected before provider work. One automatic recovery is allowed after interruption, after which a member must start a new search.
- The current one-process EC2 deployment is deliberate. S3 stores documents and backups, while transactional outreach state remains on the encrypted application volume. Measure queue age, database lock time, provider use, and host memory before adding workers or PostgreSQL.
- A Find people run treats `max_prospects` (UI "How many people", default 60) as when to stop searching, not a limit on people: every qualified person a run finds is saved, even past the target. After the first pass it runs extra rounds of new queries until the target is met, a round adds fewer than `DISCOVERY_ROUND_MIN_NEW` (2), `DISCOVERY_MAX_ROUNDS` (4) extra rounds are spent, or `DISCOVERY_RUN_BUDGET_SEC` (240) elapses. People who fail verification are replaced by more searching, never let through to reach the number. A shortfall is recorded in `research_json.shortfall` with a reason: `exhausted` (the sources had nobody more), `round_cap`, `time_budget` or `search_failed` (the search stopped, not the company). Worst case is about 43 TinyFish searches per run (`DISCOVERY_ROUND_QUERIES` per extra round). Discovery searches do not use `reserve_tinyfish_call`; TinyFish's shared upstream limit and the builder's club-wide controls bound them.
- Every run records where its time went in `research_json.timings` (seconds, one decimal): `domain_lookup`, `website_crawl` (includes waiting for the crawl slot), `web_search` (first wave), `company_meta`, `roster`, `name_seeds`, `sources` (the wall time of those five running side by side), `merge`, then per verification pass `verify` (the whole pass) split into `identity`, `email_verify` and `ai_review` (the last two run side by side, each measured to its last progress report), `save`, and `web_search_rounds` (later rounds, which overlap verification). `gate_wait` is the time spent waiting for a `DISCOVERY_JOBS` slot, summed over the run's callers. Stages overlap, so they add up to more than `total`, the run's whole wall time. `first_prospect_s` is the seconds from the start of the run to the first saved person.
- **Who to look for.** A run carries `level` (`working` = team leads, the default; `executive`; `board`) in `research_json`, set from the page's "Who to look for". The goal counts only saved people at that level (`company_register.fit_level`, the same rule as the page's "too senior": chief officers, presidents, founders, EVPs/SVPs and board seats are never team leads). Everyone else who passes verification is still saved, ranked 15 points lower. A team-lead run's first wave asks for directors, heads, senior managers, managers, leads and functional managers (strategy, operations, partnerships, business development, innovation), not CEO/CFO or "leadership team" pages; its later rounds use team-lead title variants only. Titles typed by the member become one OR group (`("Director of" OR "Head of")`). Runs queued before this field existed count as team-lead runs.
- Web search prefers TinyFish through Monid and falls back to Firecrawl. It calls TinyFish's free endpoints, `/free/search` and `/free/fetch`, which cost $0 and need no wallet balance. Monid now charges for the plain `/search` ($0.005 a call) and `/fetch` ($0.001 a result), which answer 402 on an empty wallet, so do not switch back to them. A 402, a 401/403 key refusal, a 429 rate limit, a timeout or another provider failure is not an empty search: `web_fetch` classifies it, `web_contact_discovery.search_errors_summary` carries one member-safe sentence, and the company lane says the search is paused or unavailable rather than "Nobody found." Credit, authentication and rate failures briefly suppress repeated Monid calls in-process. Admin → Operations shows the last real Monid state without making a probe call.
- A person found through web search is kept only when their own headline names the company, or one of its group units, as their employer; company-site and SEC/Companies House roster people keep their own evidence. Everyday-word brands (Amazon, Apple, Meta, Target) must be the employer exactly; short acronym firms may lead a division ("IBM Consulting", "KPMG Advisory", "EY-Parthenon"). Group-unit people are tagged `evidence_json.group_unit`. Shared mailboxes (info@, sales@, careers@, …) are rejected by `contact_scraper.is_role_based_local_part` everywhere.
- The **catalog builder** (`services/catalog_builder.py`, Admin → Operations, off by default; club setting `builder_enabled`) works down the company register in the background: SEC-listed companies in market-cap order (the first 500 approximate the S&P 500; the register has no exchange, so "then Nasdaq" is the rest of SEC-listed by rank), then Companies House GROUP and FULL filers, then everything else in the register's value order. A 60-second tick queues one company at a time as an ordinary discovery run (`origin='builder'`, target `BUILDER_TARGET_PEOPLE`, 60) owned by a `system`-role account that no session, API key or Google sign-in resolves to. It skips companies anyone searched within `BUILDER_RECRAWL_DAYS` (30) and pauses while the club has made `BUILDER_MAX_SEARCHES_PER_HOUR` (250) or `BUILDER_MAX_SEARCHES_PER_DAY` (6000) real upstream searches, or has reserved more than `BUILDER_MAX_AI_SHARE` (0.5) of the hourly Bedrock budget. The drain claims member runs before any builder run and never takes a builder run while a member run waits. The run's own verification and AI review trim what it finds; people stay in `yucgoutreach_prospects`, shown on the Find page to every member who opens that company (the newest run per company wins), and reach contacts only when a member keeps them. Progress is kept in `catalog_builder_companies`.
- **Teammates' searches are shared.** `GET /api/yucgoutreach/runs?companies=…` returns, besides the member's own runs, the newest run per open company that someone else's work makes shared: a finished builder run, or a finished member run by a still-active member that found people and completed within `SHARED_RUN_DAYS` (30). A teammate's run carries `shared_from: {name, completed_at}`; the Find page shows it at once as "Found by <name> · <date>" with **Search again**, which starts the member's own run as ever. `GET /runs/{id}`, its `/prospects`, `/export.xlsx` and `/import-contacts` accept a run when it is the member's own, a builder run, or a teammate's run meeting those same conditions (`_SHARED_MEMBER_RUN`); failed, empty, older, still-running and deactivated members' runs stay private to their owner, and only the owner deletes a run. While a run works, the page polls it (2.5 s) and shows the people it has saved so far, reading them again whenever `prospects_count` rises; the worker saves a verification round's survivors together, so people appear per round, not per person.
- **AI refusal and speed.** When AWS refuses Bedrock for the whole account (`Error 002: Access to Bedrock models is not allowed for this account`, since 2026-11-06), `llm._gated` treats it like a model not enabled: the budget reservation is released and the model is skipped for 15 minutes. Contact review checks `llm.refused_everywhere` and returns "unreviewed" at once instead of retrying and splitting batches. Measured 2026-11-09 on a copy of prod: the review stage fell from 28–58 s to under 1 s per search, and the first person was saved 8 s into a search instead of 34–41 s. Two crawl slots at once (`DISCOVERY_JOBS=2`) gave no reliable gain in the same test, because search-provider latency dominates, so the default stays one slot below 3 GiB.
- **Catalog builder hours.** The builder only starts a company inside `BUILDER_HOURS_UTC` (default `1-11`, 8 pm-6 am Eastern), because a started run holds the crawl slot until it ends and members search during the day.

## Contact directory

The Directory page (`/directory`, Outreach menu; `routers/directory.py`, `services/contact_directory.py`) is the club's one list of every known person: their best address, how well it is proven, and whether anyone has written to them. Email, lowercased, is the only key the sources share, so a person is one row per address.

- **Who is listed.** Contacts the viewer may see (members: their own plus unowned; administrators: all, the `list_contacts` rule) plus prospects from runs the viewer may read (own, builder, and teammates' finished runs within `SHARED_RUN_DAYS`; `_SHARED_MEMBER_RUN`). Where an address is both a contact and a prospect, the contact row wins and its blank name, title, company and source are filled from the prospect. Another member's private contact, a running or older teammate run and a failed run never appear; this is enforced in the query, not the page.
- **Address proof.** `mailbox_verdicts.proof_for`: `replied`, `bounced`, `mailbox_rejected`, `delivered`, `mailbox_confirmed`, `catch_all`, `unverified`.
- **Reach.** `contact_directory.reach_for(db, emails, viewer_id)` is the single definition of whether the club has reached an address: `replied` > `bounced` > `contacted` > `queued` (pending in a campaign) > `not_contacted`. A send by any member in any campaign counts (the send ledger, `outreach_messages` per row), and so does a member moving the contact to Contacted/Closed (contacted) or Replied/Meeting (replied) by hand. It returns who (member name; `by_me` for the viewer), when the last email went, how many were sent, and `claimed_by` when another member holds the company (`company_claims`, live claims only).
- **Ready to contact** (`ready=1`): proof `delivered` or `mailbox_confirmed`, reach `not_contacted` (nobody sent or queued), and no other member holds the company. A catch-all domain proves nothing about the person, and replied or bounced people are not ready.
- **Paging and filters.** `GET /api/directory?q=&company=&proof=&reach=&ready=1&limit=&offset=` (limit 1-200) searches and pages in SQL. Proof, reach and ready are properties of the joined evidence, so those filters are computed for everyone matching the text (up to `SCAN_LIMIT`, 20,000, newest first) and paged afterwards, never on an already-cut page; past the limit the answer says `capped: true` and the page says so. `GET /api/directory/summary` counts people per proof and reach (same limit) and returns `backlog_status`; `GET /api/directory/export` is the current filter as CSV (cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` are prefixed with `'`). The header line shows the verification backlog: confirmed addresses, checks used today of the daily cap, and addresses waiting.

## Company register

The register (`company_register`, Find people → Companies) holds one row per government record: SEC listing, Reg D filing, benefit-plan filing, IRS nonprofit return, Companies House, and the club sheet (`club_targets`). Members work with companies, so the list shows one entry per company.

- **Public name.** `company_name` stays the legal name as filed and is never rewritten. `display_name` is the public name: legal suffixes dropped (Inc, Corp, Company, LLC, Ltd, Limited, LP, LLP, PLC, PLLC, PC), casing repaired (`Apple (Uk) Limited` → `Apple (UK)`, ALL-CAPS names title-cased with real acronyms kept), and a reviewed override from `company_entity.ENTITY_OVERRIDES` applied (`PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP` → `PwC`). An override renames a record only on the curated company's exact legal name (`Citigroup Inc.` → `Citi`), on a nickname of two or more words in the company's home country (`Barclays Bank PLC` → `Barclays`), or, for the worldwide professional networks, on a distinctive spelling of five letters or more. One-word nicknames such as `Chase`, `Fidelity` or `Citi` never rename, because they also name unrelated companies. The legal name is visible only in the records log.
- **One entry per company.** `entity_key` is `<country>:<normalised public name>` (lowercase, punctuation dropped, leading "the" and legal suffixes dropped). Rows sharing it are one company whatever their tier, source or state, so a chapter filed in forty states under the parent's name is one entry and the log lists each state. A tier filter lists a company when any of its records is that kind; the entry still shows every kind it is as badges.
- **Hidden entries.** `hidden_reason` marks investment funds (fund numbering such as "Fund II", "Income Fund", "Fund LP"), acquisition vehicles (Bidco/Holdco/Topco/Midco/Finco/Propco/Newco, SPV, merger sub, "Acquisition Corp"), nominee and trustee companies, and IRS group returns ("… Chartered In The State Of …"). The rules are in `services/register_names.hidden_reason`; each needs a signal only a vehicle has, because a false hide drops a real organisation (`Fund for the Public Interest`, `Children's Defense Fund` and `Trustees of Boston College` stay visible). Hidden rows stay stored and searchable: `GET /api/yucgoutreach/register?include_hidden=true`.
- **Records log.** `GET /api/yucgoutreach/register/records?key=<entity_key>` lists one line per stored record with a plain source label, date, state, the legal name as filed, amounts and a public link when the record has one. Amounts appear only there, never in the list. `GET …/register/entity-people?key=` returns everyone named on any record, once each; `POST …/register/{id}/people` reads a listed or UK company's own register on demand.
- **One shared derivation.** `register_names.derive` computes all three columns. Every ingest path (SEC listing, Form D, Form 5500, IRS 990, Companies House, the club sheet, the off-box loader) ends in `company_register.upsert_companies`, which calls it; nothing else writes a company name. Bump `DERIVATION_VERSION` when any rule changes.
- **Backfill and blue/green.** The columns (`display_name`, `entity_key`, `hidden_reason`, `derived_version`, `size_rank`) are nullable additions made by `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN`, so the previous release runs beside this one. `backfill_register_entities` fills rows where `derived_version` is NULL or older than `DERIVATION_VERSION`, in batches of 2,000 rows each in its own transaction. It is registered through `leader_only` as the `company_register_derive` interval job (30 seconds after start, then every 5 minutes), never from `init_db` or a request, and reads only the `derived_version` index when nothing is left. Until a row is derived the company list leaves it out (the summary reports it as `pending`); rows the previous release inserts during an overlap are picked up by the next tick. A legal-name change made by the previous release on an already-derived row is not re-derived until the version is bumped.
- **Performance.** The list groups by `entity_key` over one covering index (`idx_company_register_entity`), so a page and its count are one pass over the index. Measured on a laptop with 217,000 synthetic rows: the default first page takes about 0.15 s and a tier filter 0.02–0.12 s. A typed search scanned every name (about 0.5–0.9 s); it now narrows candidates with the name index below.
- **Name index.** Migration `outreach:2` creates `company_register_fts`, a contentless FTS5 table with the trigram tokenizer over `company_name`, `display_name` and `sector_label`, plus insert, update and delete triggers on `company_register` (so either release's writes keep it current). The migration indexes no existing row: the leader-only `company_register_index` job (`index_register_names`, 45 seconds after start, then every 5 minutes) indexes the rows that exist in batches of 2,000 by id and then sets the `register_fts_ready` setting; until then, and for queries under three characters, search keeps the `LIKE` scan. Once ready, `_search_clauses` adds `id IN (trigram matches UNION ticker matches)` in front of the unchanged `LIKE` tests and relevance `CASE`, so matches and their order are exactly the scan's; a short query's punctuation gaps (`at&t`) are looked up as each word-break character. Measured on a laptop with 200,000 synthetic rows: a selective query (a ticker, an exact or multi-word name, a brand alias) fell from about 0.5–0.9 s to 0.02–0.08 s; a query matching many thousands of rows (`metal`, `software`) from about 0.6 s to 0.2 s; two-letter queries are unchanged. The index adds about 30 MB per 200,000 rows (about 150 bytes a row, so roughly 33–50 MB for the 217,000-row register) and indexing them takes about 30 s in total.
- **Callers.** Find people, the campaign pipeline and the target-list/release flow receive the public name for display; register ids stay the key for release targets and officer lookups. `campaigns._company_notes` and the analytics sector joins match a company by public or legal name; the catalog builder skips hidden rows and any company whose other record was crawled, and resolves the public name.

## Required regression evidence

`backend/tests/test_register_entities.py` proves the public-name and entity-key table (suffixes, casing, UK and LP names, the PwC override), the hide rules with the real organisations they must not hide, the grouped list (one row per company, any-record tier filter, hidden excluded, pagination, public-name search), the records log, every ingest path storing the derived columns, and an idempotent, version-aware backfill. `frontend/tests/company-register.spec.ts` covers the list, badges, log and plain tier labels.

`backend/tests/test_outreach_pipeline.py` proves duplicate-add idempotency, legacy duplicate quarantine, private-contact and target-list isolation, terminal bounce preservation, reconnect-safe retries, per-campaign scheduler isolation, one-active-search admission, active-member enforcement, fenced job claiming, and bounded restart recovery without calling Gmail or discovery providers.

`backend/tests/test_directory.py` proves one row per address across contacts and prospects, that a member never sees another member's private contact or an unshared run, every reach state with who and when (queued, contacted by a teammate, replied, bounced), the company-claim flag, ready-filter correctness, filtered paging totals, summary counts and the CSV export. `frontend/tests/directory.spec.ts` covers filters, reach text, the backlog line, the empty and error states and the export link.

The broader gates run the isolated backend suite, frontend lint/build and Playwright checks, infrastructure tests, security scans, and image verification before branch promotion.
