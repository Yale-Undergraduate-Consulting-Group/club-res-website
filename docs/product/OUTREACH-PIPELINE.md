# Outreach pipeline

This is the operational contract for finding contacts, preparing personalized mail, sending from member accounts, and reporting outcomes.

## Member path

1. **Target lists** select companies from the club prospect dataset.
2. **People** records proposed contacts, evidence, and keep/drop decisions. Kept contacts link directly into Drafts with the target-list filter preserved.
3. **Find contacts** creates a durable, member-owned company search. Website and permitted provider evidence are merged, addresses are normalized, and mail routing is checked. Import moves accepted results into the club contact catalog.
4. **Drafts** stores drafts under the current member. A bulk template must use explicit fields such as `{{first_name}}` or `{{company}}`; otherwise the open editor is applied only to its current recipient and other recipients keep their own saved drafts.
5. **Campaigns** copies one subject and body per recipient. The owner previews, repairs, or removes each recipient before release.
6. **Release** freezes the sender, recipient, content, signature, and follow-up steps. Subsequent contact edits cannot retarget a released message.
7. **Dispatch** claims a small batch transactionally and sends through the campaign owner's Gmail OAuth account. A provider timeout remains quarantined for reconciliation. A rejection before submission is safe to retry after the member fixes Gmail access.
8. **Results** attaches opens, replies, delivery delays, and bounces to the exact outbound message. Open pixels are approximate; replies and Gmail delivery notifications are stronger evidence.

## Address confidence

The application can prove address syntax and whether a domain has a mail route without sending mail. Optional SMTP `RCPT TO` probing never issues `DATA`, but many corporate servers block probes or accept every recipient. Those outcomes remain **mailbox unconfirmed**. Only a later delivery, bounce, or reply supplies operational mailbox evidence.

The UI and API must not label MX, inferred patterns, AI output, or an accepted SMTP probe as inbox verified.

This matches the boundary in the SMTP standard: receiving systems may disable `VRFY` for privacy and return `252` when they cannot verify a user. YAMM likewise learns its useful states after a campaign is sent: its official tracking report covers opens, clicks, replies, bounces, and unsubscribes. The club implementation therefore uses local syntax, DNS/MX, source evidence, and optional no-`DATA` probing before send, then uses the tracking pixel and the member's Gmail metadata after send. See [RFC 5321](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5321/), [YAMM tracking documentation](https://yamm.com/help/), and [Google's Gmail sending guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126).

## Scale and ownership

- Contacts without an owner are shared club catalog records. An assigned contact is visible to its member and administrators.
- Drafts, discovery runs, campaigns, Gmail credentials, immutable dispatches, and reconciliation are member-bound.
- Duplicate additions resolve to one campaign recipient. A recipient can produce only one initial dispatch key.
- One failed or inactive sender cannot stop other members' scheduled campaign batches.
- Initial sends reserve against a configurable member-wide daily ceiling (`CAMPAIGN_DAILY_SEND_LIMIT`, default 100), including uncertain sends. Parallel campaigns cannot multiply that allowance.
- The club-wide Bedrock reservation ceiling also covers discovery ranking, so contact research cannot bypass the inference budget used by Studio.
- Discovery runs use the SQLite-backed queue. One active run per member and a bounded club queue protect the single EC2 host. Expiring fenced leases prevent overlapping workers from writing the same run; inactive members are rejected before provider work. One automatic recovery is allowed after interruption, after which a member must start a new search.
- The current one-process EC2 deployment is deliberate. S3 stores documents and backups, while transactional outreach state remains on the encrypted application volume. Measure queue age, database lock time, provider use, and host memory before adding workers or PostgreSQL.
- A Find people run treats `max_prospects` (UI "How many people", default 60) as when to stop searching, not a limit on people: every qualified person a run finds is saved, even past the target. After the first pass it runs extra rounds of new queries until the target is met, a round adds fewer than `DISCOVERY_ROUND_MIN_NEW` (2), `DISCOVERY_MAX_ROUNDS` (4) extra rounds are spent, or `DISCOVERY_RUN_BUDGET_SEC` (240) elapses. People who fail verification are replaced by more searching, never let through to reach the number. A shortfall is recorded in `research_json.shortfall` with a reason: `exhausted` (the sources had nobody more), `round_cap`, `time_budget` or `search_failed` (the search stopped, not the company). Worst case is about 43 TinyFish searches per run (`DISCOVERY_ROUND_QUERIES` per extra round). Discovery searches do not use `reserve_tinyfish_call`; TinyFish's shared upstream limit, the funded Monid wallet and the builder's club-wide controls bound them.
- Web search prefers TinyFish through Monid and falls back to Firecrawl. A 402 empty wallet, 401/403 key refusal, 429 rate limit, timeout or provider failure is not an empty search: `web_fetch` classifies it, `web_contact_discovery.search_errors_summary` carries one member-safe sentence, and the company lane says the search is paused or unavailable rather than "Nobody found." Credit, authentication and rate failures briefly suppress repeated Monid calls in-process. Admin → Operations shows the last real Monid state without making a paid probe. Monid must have wallet credit; the live 2026-11-06 refusal estimated about $0.005 for one search.
- A person found through web search is kept only when their own headline names the company, or one of its group units, as their employer; company-site and SEC/Companies House roster people keep their own evidence. Everyday-word brands (Amazon, Apple, Meta, Target) must be the employer exactly; short acronym firms may lead a division ("IBM Consulting", "KPMG Advisory", "EY-Parthenon"). Group-unit people are tagged `evidence_json.group_unit`. Shared mailboxes (info@, sales@, careers@, …) are rejected by `contact_scraper.is_role_based_local_part` everywhere.
- The **catalog builder** (`services/catalog_builder.py`, Admin → Operations, off by default; club setting `builder_enabled`) works down the company register in the background: SEC-listed companies in market-cap order (the first 500 approximate the S&P 500; the register has no exchange, so "then Nasdaq" is the rest of SEC-listed by rank), then Companies House GROUP and FULL filers, then everything else in the register's value order. A 60-second tick queues one company at a time as an ordinary discovery run (`origin='builder'`, target `BUILDER_TARGET_PEOPLE`, 60) owned by a `system`-role account that no session, API key or Google sign-in resolves to. It skips companies anyone searched within `BUILDER_RECRAWL_DAYS` (30) and pauses while the club has made `BUILDER_MAX_SEARCHES_PER_HOUR` (250) or `BUILDER_MAX_SEARCHES_PER_DAY` (6000) real upstream searches, or has reserved more than `BUILDER_MAX_AI_SHARE` (0.5) of the hourly Bedrock budget. The drain claims member runs before any builder run and never takes a builder run while a member run waits. The run's own verification and AI review trim what it finds; people stay in `yucgoutreach_prospects`, shown on the Find page to every member who opens that company (the newest run per company wins), and reach contacts only when a member keeps them. Progress is kept in `catalog_builder_companies`.

## Company register

The register (`company_register`, Find people → Companies) holds one row per government record: SEC listing, Reg D filing, benefit-plan filing, IRS nonprofit return, Companies House, and the club sheet (`club_targets`). Members work with companies, so the list shows one entry per company.

- **Public name.** `company_name` stays the legal name as filed and is never rewritten. `display_name` is the public name: legal suffixes dropped (Inc, Corp, Company, LLC, Ltd, Limited, LP, LLP, PLC, PLLC, PC), casing repaired (`Apple (Uk) Limited` → `Apple (UK)`, ALL-CAPS names title-cased with real acronyms kept), and a reviewed override from `company_entity.ENTITY_OVERRIDES` applied (`PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP` → `PwC`). An override renames a record only on the curated company's exact legal name (`Citigroup Inc.` → `Citi`), on a nickname of two or more words in the company's home country (`Barclays Bank PLC` → `Barclays`), or, for the worldwide professional networks, on a distinctive spelling of five letters or more. One-word nicknames such as `Chase`, `Fidelity` or `Citi` never rename, because they also name unrelated companies. The legal name is visible only in the records log.
- **One entry per company.** `entity_key` is `<country>:<normalised public name>` (lowercase, punctuation dropped, leading "the" and legal suffixes dropped). Rows sharing it are one company whatever their tier, source or state, so a chapter filed in forty states under the parent's name is one entry and the log lists each state. A tier filter lists a company when any of its records is that kind; the entry still shows every kind it is as badges.
- **Hidden entries.** `hidden_reason` marks investment funds (fund numbering such as "Fund II", "Income Fund", "Fund LP"), acquisition vehicles (Bidco/Holdco/Topco/Midco/Finco/Propco/Newco, SPV, merger sub, "Acquisition Corp"), nominee and trustee companies, and IRS group returns ("… Chartered In The State Of …"). The rules are in `services/register_names.hidden_reason`; each needs a signal only a vehicle has, because a false hide drops a real organisation (`Fund for the Public Interest`, `Children's Defense Fund` and `Trustees of Boston College` stay visible). Hidden rows stay stored and searchable: `GET /api/yucgoutreach/register?include_hidden=true`.
- **Records log.** `GET /api/yucgoutreach/register/records?key=<entity_key>` lists one line per stored record with a plain source label, date, state, the legal name as filed, amounts and a public link when the record has one. Amounts appear only there, never in the list. `GET …/register/entity-people?key=` returns everyone named on any record, once each; `POST …/register/{id}/people` reads a listed or UK company's own register on demand.
- **One shared derivation.** `register_names.derive` computes all three columns. Every ingest path (SEC listing, Form D, Form 5500, IRS 990, Companies House, the club sheet, the off-box loader) ends in `company_register.upsert_companies`, which calls it; nothing else writes a company name. Bump `DERIVATION_VERSION` when any rule changes.
- **Backfill and blue/green.** The columns (`display_name`, `entity_key`, `hidden_reason`, `derived_version`, `size_rank`) are nullable additions made by `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN`, so the previous release runs beside this one. `backfill_register_entities` fills rows where `derived_version` is NULL or older than `DERIVATION_VERSION`, in batches of 2,000 rows each in its own transaction. It is registered through `leader_only` as the `company_register_derive` interval job (30 seconds after start, then every 5 minutes), never from `init_db` or a request, and reads only the `derived_version` index when nothing is left. Until a row is derived the company list leaves it out (the summary reports it as `pending`); rows the previous release inserts during an overlap are picked up by the next tick. A legal-name change made by the previous release on an already-derived row is not re-derived until the version is bumped.
- **Performance.** The list groups by `entity_key` over one covering index (`idx_company_register_entity`), so a page and its count are one pass over the index. Measured on a laptop with 217,000 synthetic rows: the default first page takes about 0.15 s, a tier filter 0.02–0.12 s, and a typed search about 0.3 s (it scans names). The one-time backfill of 217,000 rows takes about 15 s.
- **Callers.** Find people, the campaign pipeline and the target-list/release flow receive the public name for display; register ids stay the key for release targets and officer lookups. `campaigns._company_notes` and the analytics sector joins match a company by public or legal name; the catalog builder skips hidden rows and any company whose other record was crawled, and resolves the public name.

## Required regression evidence

`backend/tests/test_register_entities.py` proves the public-name and entity-key table (suffixes, casing, UK and LP names, the PwC override), the hide rules with the real organisations they must not hide, the grouped list (one row per company, any-record tier filter, hidden excluded, pagination, public-name search), the records log, every ingest path storing the derived columns, and an idempotent, version-aware backfill. `frontend/tests/company-register.spec.ts` covers the list, badges, log and plain tier labels.

`backend/tests/test_outreach_pipeline.py` proves duplicate-add idempotency, legacy duplicate quarantine, private-contact and target-list isolation, terminal bounce preservation, reconnect-safe retries, per-campaign scheduler isolation, one-active-search admission, active-member enforcement, fenced job claiming, and bounded restart recovery without calling Gmail or discovery providers.

The broader gates run the isolated backend suite, frontend lint/build and Playwright checks, infrastructure tests, security scans, and image verification before branch promotion.
